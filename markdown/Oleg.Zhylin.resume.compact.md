## Most prominent achievements

### Embedded devices for health and safety

* ***Architecture Ownership**, from the first device to the portfolio*. In 2018 I joined a brilliant team building the [Lively Mobile+][r4] emergency-response device, and wrote original **Embedded** software for it. Radio waves and technology stacks are deprived of a sense of urgency to save a human life, so the engineering has to supply it. I used *modern C++* and scalable architectures through multiple *successful Product Launches*, and took ownership well beyond my assigned area: *Performance*, *Testability*, outstanding *Battery Life* and *Operational Excellence*. Since **April 2026** that ownership covers **Architecture** decisions for the **Wearables** ([Lively Mobile+][r4], [Lively Mobile 2][r5]) and the **Handsets** (*Jitterbug* flip and smart phones). I use **AI-assisted** *Rapid Prototyping* to get productive on a new stack fast, and to judge an idea's **Innovation Potential** before it costs a team a quarter.
<!-- include: _parts/bullet-retail-shelf.md -->
* ***Concurrency** and **Network Programming**, the fundamentals of embedded work*. A backend engineer in **Python**, **Go**, **Java** or **.NET** inherits a runtime that already schedules the work and speaks the protocol. On embedded hardware the engineer *is* that runtime, both on the bare-metal **sensor co-processor MCU** (**RTOS** queues and timers, the wake-up chain, the protocol between the two processors) and on the hosted **Embedded Linux** processor (threads, **wakelocks**, **IPC** buses, **MQTT** sync cadence). I learned it the expensive way, building a cross-platform **TCP/IP** server single-handedly in 2004. Now I apply it to other people's code: I diagnosed an intermittent race condition in a device's critcal service process without ever reproducing it, then drove a contract manufacturer's concurrency corrections round by round until QA reported the service running smoothly. **[Boost.Asio][boost_asio]** is my day-to-day abstraction, and I value it because I built its hand-rolled equivalent first.
* **Positioning Technologies** (**GNSS** — GPS, GLONASS, Galileo — ECID, Wi-Fi, **BLE** beacons). Accurate **Location** fixes are critical for an emergency-response device. I became the company's **Subject Matter Expert on Positioning**, diagnosed and fixed implementation issues, and designed and executed a *major upgrade* of the Positioning infrastructure. For [Lively Mobile 2][r5] I upgraded positioning via Qualcomm Skyhook on Qualcomm Linux Enablement, then architected the **Location Engine**: beacon, GPS and Wi-Fi behind per-provider interfaces, with a state-management layer that arbitrates between sources and falls back when one fails. I have owned **BLE beacon** home-or-away tracking since its inception.
* **Fully Automated Fall Detection**. A personal emergency-response device has to call for help by itself when its wearer falls. For [Lively Mobile+][r4] I built the infrastructure that filters the **MCU**'s signals and coordinates the device's subsystems to place that call, and a fall is tracked even if the device reboots. Fall detection is Lively Mobile's **killer feature**: the **Care** center is load-bearing, but fall detection is the focused driver. For the next-generation wearable I designed a **sensor-cluster architecture** that classifies motion in the sensor's own **machine-learning core**, so the application processor stays asleep. As architecture owner I am now driving the next round of **fall-detection innovation** for active seniors.
* ***Regulated Medical Devices***. I asked for the [Current Health][current_health] *Hospital at Home* work more than a year before it came, and earned the organization's trust to take it on. I qualified into the remote patient monitoring platform's medical-device **Quality Management System** — **Design Control**, **CAPA**, **Supplier Quality** and product release, under **FDA**, **EU** and **Australian** regulatory process — and in record time **mastered [Orcanos][orcanos]**, the eQMS/ALM that regulated development actually runs through. I mastered a **PPG** (photoplethysmography) wearable just as quickly, the optical sensing behind heart rate and related vitals. On the engineering side I traced a firmware defect end to end: a default temperature value was reaching the telemetry payload and making the platform conclude that the device was not on the patient.
* ***Embedded Platform Foundations***. I led the design and delivery of an embedded **component framework** that standardizes how every device SKU declares, configures and brings up its features: **dependency injection**, **hierarchical state machines** with predictable lifecycle management, and an aligned **structured logging** framework, all in embedded **C**, modularized and documented for **open-source** release. For a safety-critical embedded **C++** codebase I chose the coding standard by what static analysis could actually check, comparing **MISRA C++**, **JSF** and the **[C++ Core Guidelines][core_guidelines]**, then authored the guidelines so the rules are verified on every build instead of remembered by reviewers. I also designed the **[Conan][conan]** package versioning and channel-promotion policy, with binaries on **[JFrog Artifactory][artifactory]**, and established **ARM** cross-compilation.
<!-- include: _parts/bullet-operational-excellence.md -->
* ***Data Engineering**, from consulting cases to a device recall*. Through the August 2019 [CPSC recall][cpsc_recall] of Lively Mobile+ and the relaunch that followed, existing tooling could not say, at fleet scale, which devices were affected or whether a fix had taken. My **Data Engineering** on device and server telemetry turned that from argument into measurement, and the analysis outlived the crisis and changed how the product was maintained. I have taken on **Data Preparation** at every job and in every project; **[Data Wrangling][data_wrangling]** easily gobbles up 80% of the time and budget between raw data and a valuable insight. I prepared data for consulting projects, troubleshooting, forensics and analytical support cases, with results ready in days or even hours, and the **ETL** I built let us iterate as many times as the answer required.

### How I work

* ***Integration**, and the **engineer-to-engineer** relationships it runs on*. Integration is the work I keep being drawn to: two device lines meeting in one care experience, a device programme meeting a contract manufacturer, a regulated medical-device culture meeting a consumer one. On the *Hospital at Home* platform I pushed to bring [Lively Mobile 2][r5]'s **fall detection**, a **PPG** wearable and the surrounding sensors into one experience, because overlapping radios and vitals give a home without a nurse in the next room a second, independent path to the same fact. I also know where the instinct stops. Building a device **ground-up** keeps the fix in-house, at hardware's pace, and integrating through a thin **white-label** interface keeps the vendor replaceable. I have run the first and studied the second at close range, and my read is that the trap lies in the **hybrid**: too specialized to switch vendors, and not owned closely enough to fix in-house. Hardware runs on a rigid cycle of six months or more while the software iterates inside it, and my best results across that seam came from engineer-to-engineer relationships with manufacturer, silicon and firmware partners.
* ***Stewardship**, practised by shifting quality left and eliminating toil*. Good stewardship and a strong sense of ownership are my first principles. As an engineer I keep inherited architectures sound after their authors move on, and I took charge of the architecture for **automated tests that run on the device**, cutting test time and raising quality by orders of magnitude, then onboarded the **QA** engineers to run it so the gains outlived my attention. As a **Security Professional** I drafted a **[NIST][nist]**-grounded patch-management SOP for a device fleet, and proposed **risk appetite** as the foundation of an organization's **Risk Management**. With data, I am an official **Data Steward** on the enterprise data catalog (**Alation**) for device data. I believe a steward is judged by what is handed on. Hand work is where drift and single points of knowledge live, so I automate it away.
* ***Management**, **Technical Leadership**, and the people who make **Agile** work*. The **Agile Manifesto** puts *individuals and interactions* first, and so do I: my best results came from **motivated, self-organizing teams** and from **empowering others**. At Salford Systems I managed teams of up to **15 people**, coordinating **U.S.** developers with **Outsourcing** contractors in **Ukraine**, **Poland** and **China**. I held the contractors to the same standard as staff and gave them the support to reach it, and they became instrumental contributors to features and quality rather than extra capacity. When I raise the bar, environments differ: one team over-performs, another is surprised and then glad to aim higher, a third is careful by temperament. I read which is which and adapt instead of insisting. I learned early that a result nobody hears about does not exist, so I make every result known, then hand it on so the people who receive it can own it. I **lead from behind**, and the credit goes to the people who carry the work forward.

### Machine learning, before the mainstream

* ***Machine Learning products**, from the desktop to the cloud*. Serving the whole range of users, from **Domain Experts** far from statistics to the best **Data Scientists** in the world, was at the core of Salford Systems' business. I was the primary **Graphical User Interface (GUI)** developer for the flagship [Salford Predictive Modeler (SPM)][spm82] across releases from [CART 4.0][cart_4_0] to [SPM 8.2][spm82], and those GUIs brought solid success to several consecutive releases, funding further Machine Learning innovation. I grew into **Chief Architect** and **Product Owner** of the **[Qt][qt]** GUI rewrite, a **Principal Architect** and the Product Owner of **Cloud-ready SPM** (**OpenAPI**, **React**/**Redux**, and a **Python** backend on a **Redis** distributed queue), and architect of the **Machine Learning engines API**, built test-first so that other teams could build cloud products and customer pilots on it. As an architect I set a **strong vision** and made the decisions together with *engineering*, *business* and *scientific* teams.
* ***Big Data** and **Distributed Machine Learning***. From 2014 to 2017 I led the technology path for Salford Systems' distributed machine learning — **Hadoop** and **PySpark**, a **[Databricks][databricks]** partnership, then **[Dask][dask]** — in **groundbreaking research** on **peta-scale datasets**. With my co-architect I built the **hybrid cloud** it ran on: every workload in **containers** under **[Rancher][rancher]**, across our own machines and **AWS**, with the data and the model-building workers in both.
* ***Machine Learning engines from the inside**, and legacy code*. Long before an AI assistant could explain an unfamiliar numerical routine, I debugged the classic **Machine Learning** implementations, **CART**, **TreeNet** and **MARS**, at their computational core: mathematical papers encoded in **Fortran** and **C/C++**. Telling a numerical bug from a statistical property meant learning the mathematics and working alongside the statisticians who owned it. It tested my mathematical boundaries and taught me to value the talent on the other side of them. I also took [SPM][spm82], a memory-hungry product, to **64 bits**, hardened it for **Unicode**, and kept **Technical Debt** to a pragmatic **minimum** in that codebase and others.
* ***Custody of a company's engineering through its acquisition***. Before [Minitab][minitab] acquired Salford Systems I led engineering's side of its technical visits, and afterwards I carried the integration. I transferred the entire **Intellectual Property** of Salford Systems under proper **Governance**; detailed records I had kept for years are what made that pace possible. I completed the migration of codebase, issue tracking and [CI/CD][cicd] onto [Visual Studio Team Services][vsts] in **less than a year**, and when leadership asked me to scale up the team, I built the process that cut onboarding from **2-3 months to less than a week**.
