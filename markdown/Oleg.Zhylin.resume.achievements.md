### 2026. Capability and Configuration Framework.

Device SKUs had drifted into managing their capabilities each in their own way, which makes every cross-device change a survey before it is an edit. I led the design and delivery of a **Capability and Configuration Framework** that standardizes capability management across the portfolio, built in embedded **C** around **dependency injection** and **hierarchical state machines** with predictable lifecycle management, together with an aligned **structured logging** framework that gives consistent, correlatable logs across every supported device. I modularized and documented the framework for **open-source** release and external contribution.

### 2025-2026. Modular Location Engine.

Positioning on a wearable had grown into per-source special cases. I architected a **Location Engine** that unifies **BLE beacons**, **GPS** and **Wi-Fi** behind per-provider interfaces, with a state-management layer that arbitrates between sources and falls back cleanly when a provider fails, and documented the design and interfaces so other teams can extend it for new device variants.

I then designed schema-backed persistence that restores paired **beacon** state across **firmware-over-the-air** updates. Without it a device comes back from an update having forgotten its beacons and falls back to high-frequency polling; with it, devices stay in low-power presence detection, which protects battery life across the whole fleet. The same pass hardened beacon and FOTA error categorization and made **MCU** reboot and fatal-error handling deliberate rather than incidental.

### 2023-2025. Fleet device-health Observability.

Building the practice described above took a sequence of specific problems. ### 2021. Next generation wearable: power budget and dead reckoning.

Before the next wearable had a shape, I argued that the **battery budget** must be decided ahead of the form factor — making it a researchable constraint rather than one inherited from an enclosure — and made the trade-off structure explicit, including that capable hardware costs power twice: once for the part, and again for the software that uses it. I set the design goal that **Positioning** be a non-issue in the power budget.

The architecture I then wrote to satisfy it places a low-power **sensor cluster** and a dedicated **BLE MCU** between the sensors and the application processor, so the AP stays asleep, and starts the wake-up chain as low in the stack as possible by using the **machine-learning core embedded in the sensor itself** to classify motion. I selected and justified the sensor and MCU lineup against motion, fall, stair-transition, heading and **gyroscope precession** requirements, ran the component-vendor engagement, and specified a bench-evaluation programme that turned open architecture questions into measurable experiments.

### 2015. Big Data Scoring utility for *Apache Hive*.

A large Department Store chain client asked for help to apply hundreds of individual TreeNet models to billions of observations in Hadoop. They used **Apache Hive** to manage data. I was familiar with Hive before but far from an active user. A quick research brought up a nice solution. Hive supports `SELECT TRANSFORM` statement that allows specifying a custom shell script to consume data produced by Hive query.

The power of the solution comes from using [Tiny C Compiler][tcc]. It was a perfect match to distribute it in an archive to every Hadoop node and compile model implementation **on the fly** on every invocation from `SELECT TRANSFORM`.

The client liked our solution very much. It was straightforward for them to put it in production where it was used for at least one year.
