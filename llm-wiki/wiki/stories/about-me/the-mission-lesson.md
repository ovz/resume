# I raised a concern about a product vision early, and it taught me to ask how a mission is defined

## Why I still care

I was not in the room where it was decided, and I did not need to be to see it. What stays with me is not being right; it is watching good people spend real money on something that did not have a sharp enough reason to exist, and then watching the correction land on the teams who were doing well. That is why I ask the question I ask now.

**Cue:** my own one-on-one note from April 2023, written after the turn toward profitability — the list of things "that lost a ton of money", and the line after it, that the phones and devices teams "were doing a good job".

## Narrative — rehearse verbatim

**0 · Offer**
> There's one about a decision I disagreed with, early on, and what I took from it.

**1 · Hook**
> After the acquisition, the company I worked for had real resources behind it for the first time, and a vision that went well beyond the device — a whole smart-home offering for seniors.
⟨breathe⟩

**2 · Stakes**
> And to be fair, this is not a business you run on a shoestring. We were a mobile carrier on top of a national network, with a care centre and devices people rely on. It needs investment. The question is what the investment is for.

**3 · Complication**
> The vision was appealing. A retailer with installers in every city looks at one device and sees the whole home. I understood the reasoning. Mine was one engineer's opinion.
⟨breathe⟩

**4 · Move**
> I said early, and plainly, that it sounded out of touch to me. It was my expert opinion, and it was not the deciding one.
*(optional)* We were not General Magic — the famous nineties company that had every idea and not enough of a product — but some of that feeling of a rich company was there, until the money had to be accounted for.

**5 · Punchline**
> It didn't change the decision. What it taught me is what a poorly defined mission does, even to an organisation with money. So now, when I talk to an employer, I ask how the mission is defined — who the customer is, and what we will not build.
⟨breathe⟩

**6 · Handover**
> How would you describe the mission here, in one sentence?

## If they follow up

- **"Were you right?"** → The business later turned hard toward profitability, and that is public. I would not claim more than that my concern was early. The lesson is the part that is mine.
- **"How did you raise it?"** → As an engineer's opinion, in the normal channels, with my manager. I did not escalate it into a campaign, and I kept doing my own work well. My own note from 2023 says the devices teams were doing a good job.
- **"What would you do differently?"** → *(agent-drafted — keep only if it is true for you)* Put a number next to the opinion. An engineer saying "this sounds out of touch" is easy to set aside; an engineer saying "here is what the device customers actually use, and here is the cost of the new thing per customer" is harder to.
- **"Isn't this just hindsight?"** → Partly, and I try to be careful with it. That is why I tell it as the question it taught me, not as a prediction.

## Know it — what stays with me

T1: the product was **Lively Home**, GreatCall's wireless home-sensor offering installed by Geek Squad. My April 2023 note named it alongside other initiatives "that lost a ton of money and made Best Buy Health unprofitable". My August 2026 reading adds that the exclusive-brands organisation tried to fit the device line into its own way of working rather than learning to build custom devices better than anyone. **The early objection itself is undated and undocumented** — this is a story about a lesson, told at the strength the record allows. Never "every blunder I saw coming"; never a name.
