# Dream-job hub

> **Doc type:** reference
>
> The candidate directions this career could take next, each graded on what the record actually supports. **Owner-originated candidates are marked ★ and agent-suggested ones ○** — the distinction is a property on every page, visible in the Obsidian graph, and it is never quietly changed. Audience: the owner deciding where to point; an agent tailoring a resume variant, building a reading list, or judging whether a new accomplishment moves a candidate.
>

## What "dream job" means here, precisely

The owner's own definition, from the primary resume: **"a perfect combination of Innovation, Value, and Impact"** — with the stated target being a **Sr. Principal or Sr. Staff Engineer** role at an employer interested in the work rather than the titles. The three words are the owner's; the readings below are his, stated 2026-09-13, and they are narrower and harder than the obvious ones.

| | The question a candidate has to pass | Failure mode it screens out |
|---|---|---|
| **Innovation** | Is there something here that has not been built yet, that I could be the one to build? | A senior role maintaining a finished system. **"Business as usual" work becomes subject to pragmatic reasoning very quickly** — not aspirational, innovational or growth reasoning — and that is the reasoning that decides its budget |
| **Value** | Does the **organization's alpha** grow because of this work? Value is judged from the business's standpoint, never from "will I be paid for it" | A role whose expectation is that you are worth your cost. **"Pulling your own weight is a form of rent-seeking"** — every member is already assumed to bring value, so the bar is the surplus above that |
| **Impact** | Two conditions, both required: the **differential** — how much more value exists because I did the work — and **visibility**, because at this career stage impact nobody can see cannot be afforded. Plus trajectory: outcomes **strongly associated with the money-making areas** of the organization | Real value generated far from where the money is made, or generated invisibly. Note what this deliberately drops: *reaching people* is only one narrow domain in which value can be generated, and domain is no longer the criterion — magnitude is |

**The frame underneath all three.** Two axes: **technical power** and **money power**. Someone with enough technical power can build anything; money power is the business side, whose extreme — buy anything — is a perpetual-motion fantasy in the same way Laplace's demon is. Lawyers exist so that powerful people who *disagree* can still make progress; **powerful people who agree get what they want spectacularly.** The owner's strength is squarely the technical axis, and that is a self-assessment grounded in the durable record in this repository rather than a preference. So the dream job is not merely one where technical power is welcome — it is one where **technical power and money power are pointed the same way**, because that alignment is where the spectacular outcomes come from and its absence is where senior engineers spend careers being pragmatically reasoned at.

Two further constraints come from the record rather than from a wish: the owner is a **builder-architect** who stays close enough to implementation to solve the hard problem himself, and he has [argued explicitly against the management track](../../raw/brag/2025-04-01-staff-engineer-behaviors-principal-positioning.md). A candidate that requires giving up either is not a dream job, however senior.

### Where the money is — the employer decides half of every grade

The sharpest consequence of the Value and Impact readings above: **the same specialization is a different job depending on where it sits in the employer's revenue.** The `Revenue line` column below is that judgement, and it is about the *employer shape*, not the discipline:

| Revenue line | What it means | How it scores |
|---|---|---|
| **product** | The work *is* what the customer buys. Technical power and money power are structurally aligned — the engine, the algorithm, the device capability is the thing being sold | Passes Value and Impact outright. This is the target |
| **enabler** | The work makes the product possible or faster to build, but nobody buys it directly | Passes only if the differential is large and visible, and if the organization actually treats it as strategic rather than as overhead |
| **cost centre** | The work reduces what the business spends | Fails the Impact test as stated. Austerity is the reasoning that funds it, and austerity is pragmatic reasoning by definition — which is precisely the trap named in the Innovation row |

This is why two candidates with **strong** evidence can rank very differently, and it is the correction that matters most: an earlier version of this page treated "somebody pays for this" as the Value test, which would have scored a cost-reduction role as a pass.

## Mission, belonging and ownership — the register underneath the grades

> Owner's words, 2026-09-23, from a mailbox message sent by the C++ practice repository during interview preparation. **Input, not final copy**: the wording is staged for the owner's review. Tier T1 — this section frames *what he is looking for*; nothing here is for the public resume until the owner promotes a line under [resume editing](../resume/update-workflow.md).

The Innovation / Value / Impact tests above say what makes a job pass. This section says what the owner wants the job to *be*, and it reframes the target from a technology to a stance:

- **Mission-bearing work, where he finds the critical path and owns the risk on it.** Devices and systems people depend on; software whose correctness is the point. Open domains, none ranked: aerospace and defence, safety-critical devices, infrastructure, anything where people rely on the software being right.
- **Senior technical ownership among strong peers.** "Strong opinions, loosely held", with no hidden agenda; changing his mind on peer data is part of the practice. His own answer to "best peer collaboration" (2026-09-24) is the model: at Salford, he was the subject-matter expert on the company's technology and his co-architect Vlad Frolov the Python and Linux expert, with the cloud new to both — and Vlad talked him into repaving the whole in-house big-data cluster into containers, which became a hybrid AWS/on-premises platform where the cloud product built models on big data ([2016-09-01](../../raw/brag/2016-09-01-sparky-hybrid-container-cluster-rancher-aws.md)). Being persuaded by a peer strong in the other half is the evidence for the stance.
- **Judged on results and quality of collaboration, not on presence.** On-site is acceptable: five days a week is a challenge he is willing to take, and he says so rather than treating it as a cost to be negotiated away.
- **Belonging he can earn by contributing, and stewardship he can extend.** He found that sense of belonging after moving to the U.S., and the next step is to reach further with it: help on the critical path, own mission-critical risk, make the place he lives a bit stronger. The stewardship stance is already recorded as a first principle in the [stewardship cluster](../stories/stewardship.md).
- **A mission test for employer shape.** A vertical-agnostic, tools-for-anyone's-problem employer is well built but leaves him wanting a mission ("horizontal by design; I learned I need a mission"). Related lesson he now probes for explicitly: what a small company's growth actually returns to the people who build it.

**How it interacts with the grades.** It strengthens `DJ-3`, `DJ-6`, `DJ-8` and any `DJ-4` role at an employer whose product people depend on, because "people depend on the software being right" is their whole premise; it does not change the `Revenue line` screen. Treat it as an additional filter on postings, not a new candidate.

**Balance rules for any outward telling** (from the owner): do not single-focus on GPS/positioning — it is one depth among several; do not lead with feelings about the birth country — one clause at most; stay confident, forward-looking and balanced; end on what he will do, not what he felt. The stories built on this theme are the [About Me cluster](../stories/about-me.md).

### How to read the grades

- **Revenue line** — where the work sits in the employer's revenue, per the table above. This is the column the Value and Impact tests actually turn on, and it is a judgement about *employer shape*: the same candidate can be `product` at one company and `cost centre` at another, which is why several rows below are written conditionally.
- **Evidence** — how much of *this repository* supports the fit. `strong`: several brag entries or resume claims map onto the role's own vocabulary. `moderate`: the adjacent work is there, one named capability is not. `thin`: real interest and deliberate preparation, little shipped evidence. This is a statement about the record, not about ability.
- **Horizon** — `now`: could be pursued on today's record. `build`: one identified gap, closable in months by someone who eliminates toil for fun. `stretch`: needs a portfolio artifact that does not exist yet.
- Grades move when the brag file moves, not when enthusiasm moves. That is the same discipline [voice and prominence](../workflows/voice-and-prominence.md) applies backwards to accomplishments.

### What the corrected test implies

Evidence and fit are different axes, and the Value/Impact readings pull them apart. Ordering by **fit** — the decision itself stays the owner's:

1. **`DJ-2` AI sensor fusion** — the standout. Product line, strong evidence, and the owner's own idea. The only candidate that scores at the top of every axis at once.
2. **`DJ-3` resilient PNT** and **`DJ-7` telemetry engines** — both `product`, both reachable, and `DJ-7` doubles as the route to `DJ-1`.
3. **`DJ-1` database internals** — the highest alignment with the power frame there is, because at a database company the engine *is* the revenue and the two axes cannot disagree. Also the thinnest evidence. That combination is what makes it a real ambition rather than a whim, and it is why the bridge candidate exists.
4. **`DJ-9` edge AI**, then **`DJ-6` regulated medical** and **`DJ-8` Rust safety-critical** — good, conditional on the role being the device or the tooling rather than the process around it.
5. **`DJ-10` security leadership** — the owner's own idea, added 2026-09-24, and confirmed as a hub candidate on 2026-09-25 ("CSO is a dream job if I get lucky"); risk management stays a capability, not a candidate. His reasoning for CSO over CTO or CEO is on the page. `cost centre` at most employers, which the Value test penalises; `product` at an employer that sells trust, where the cryptography roots stop being a curiosity. Stretch horizon, because the gap is leading a security function rather than doing security work.
6. **`DJ-4` agentic platform** — strong evidence, but an internal platform is an `enabler`, and its differential is visible mainly to engineers. It moves up sharply at an employer that *sells* the tooling.
7. **`DJ-5` fleet reliability and cost** — the correction bites hardest here. Strong evidence, `now` horizon, and it **fails the test as stated**: cost reduction is funded by austerity reasoning, which is the pragmatic reasoning the Innovation row screens out. The owner's own contemporaneous note says the same thing from the inside — that a path-to-profitability framed as austerity is not where future margin comes from. Keep the page: the evidence in it is what makes several other candidates credible, and it is the strongest single portfolio for an employer whose *fleet is the product*.

**The pattern across all seven.** What separates the top of this list from the bottom is not the technology; it is whether the work is what a customer buys. That is a screen to apply to a job posting, not to a field.

## What every candidate is judged against

The through-line the record actually shows, which is what makes some of these easy and others a stretch:

- **Safety-critical embedded product engineering**, where reliability, battery, concurrency, positioning and emergency response all bind at once — 2018 to now, and the deepest thing he does.
- **Positioning and power as one subject**, owned rather than assigned ([positioning](../concepts/accomplishments-by-domain.md), [battery and power](../../raw/brag/2021-10-16-battery-power-second-specialization.md)).
- **Machine learning as a product discipline**, seventeen years of it, from before the vocabulary existed — which is why "AI" in a job description does not impress him and does not intimidate him either.
- **Data engineering and statistical judgement** — the owner counts the 2014–2017 Salford distributed-ML programme (Hadoop and Spark, a Databricks partnership, Dask, a hybrid-cloud container platform) as his strongest and still-current data-engineering credential ([2014-01-01](../../raw/brag/2014-01-01-cloudsml-cloudspm-bigisle-big-data-rd.md)), with relational algebra ingrained by the SQL Server years and linear algebra, in his words, not landing as smoothly — then applied to fleet telemetry, including [holding the statistical bar](../../raw/brag/2026-05-17-statistical-bar-and-data-science-partnership.md) and [finding the units that cost real money](../../raw/brag/2024-01-04-cellular-cost-rogue-device-detection.md).
- **Concurrency and the bugs that vanish when observed**, from a 2004 TCP/IP daemon to [a race condition found without ever reproducing it](../../raw/brag/2023-09-26-audio-service-race-condition-diagnosis.md).
- **Regulated process as a skill**, not as paperwork — design control, CAPA, an eQMS he can work *in*.
- **AI-assisted engineering as a team capability**, built rather than consumed.

## Adjacencies — recorded, not candidates

Real capabilities or interests that do not currently earn a page. Kept here so nobody has to rediscover them, and so a future candidate can be promoted out of this list.

- **Network engineering (routing, BGP).** The owner's own statement: he understands networking intimately from the TCP/IP daemon years onward and could pick this up if a role needed it. An aptitude, not a direction — no evidence of wanting it as the main event.
- **Formal methods (TLA+).** Studied deliberately in 2026. Reads as a *differentiator inside* the database, telemetry-engine and concurrency-heavy candidates rather than as a job of its own; the same is true of deterministic simulation testing, which is how several Rust database projects now test concurrency.
- **Data governance and stewardship.** Real and current — data-product stewardship over device event data — but it is a direction away from building, and it is a `cost centre` in revenue terms. It fails the Innovation and Impact tests together, which is a cleaner rejection than either alone.
- **Hiring, mentoring and technical interviewing.** Strong record, and it belongs in every candidate as evidence of principal-level scope, not as a destination.

## In the Obsidian graph

This directory is its own colour group, and **owner-originated candidates carry a distinct colour from suggested ones**, so the distinction the owner asked for is visible without opening anything. The hub sits at the centre of the cluster; every candidate links back here and out to the brag entries behind it, so the graph shows each direction sitting on top of its own evidence — and a candidate floating with few edges is one whose fit is asserted rather than sourced. ## Maintenance

- **The origin mark is the owner's to set.** An agent may add a candidate only as `origin: suggested`. If the owner adopts one, he changes the property and says so in the page; an agent never promotes its own suggestion.
- **One page per candidate, and the hub table is generated by hand** — keep the two consistent in the same edit.
- **Market claims live in the dated research page**, not here. When that page is refreshed, re-read the `Field` column.
- A candidate that has become the actual job is not a dream job any more: mark it `status: pursuing`, then fold it into the career record once it is real.
