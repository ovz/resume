# ★ Security leadership — an apprenticeship toward Chief Security Officer

> **Doc type:** reference · **origin: owner** — the owner's own statement, 2026-09-24: *"Official title is 26 years old, but many side-projjects that are security related, and otherwise, I resurrected Embedded from Computer Engineering Data security majjor from ukraine (not computer science based, because how things were in UA in 1990s) and I am sure I can grow into CSO role, if there is an opportunity."*

## The job

Not the CSO title tomorrow: **a security-leadership role with a path to it** — product or device security lead, security architect for a device or platform organisation, or deputy to a CISO — at an employer where security is part of what is sold or what is regulated. The word "apprenticeship" is the owner's framing: grow into the chief role by owning more of the security function each year, under someone who already holds it.

## How it relates to the risk capability

On 2026-09-22 the owner drew a line: **risk management is a capability track that belongs to his Data Security background, not a dream-job candidate** ([risk-engineering track record](../concepts/risk-engineering-track-record.md)). This candidate does not undo that. Risk practice stays a capability every candidate draws on; *security leadership* is a direction — the job whose whole content is that capability, plus the organisation around it. **The owner settled its place on 2026-09-25: it is a hub candidate** — "CSO is a dream job if I get lucky and my Cybersecurity mindset gets approeciation."

## Why this chief role and not another — the owner's view (2026-09-25, verbatim)

> This is the best specialized C-Suite job that could happen. I can be CTO just fine but variability across organizations is vast, even orgs in same narrow category. CEO is easy to define - know what is known about outside world that is relevant for the company (per Peter Drucker). So CEO is "narrow" in the sense of definition and wide in the sense of day to day duties. LIfe long learning is vital for any C-suite, the job description for CEO, but CEO has least control of what to learn, and CEO is the only person to find out what to learn.

Read plainly: of the chief roles, the CSO is the one with a *specialised* content he already holds; the CTO is within reach but means something different at every employer, even among close peers, so it cannot be aimed at in the abstract; the CEO's job is well defined (after Drucker, knowing what is known about the outside world that matters to the company) but its daily content is unbounded. Lifelong learning runs through all three — and the CEO alone has to discover what to learn, with the least control over it. This is why the candidate is the CSO path and not a general C-suite ambition. The Drucker attribution is the owner's; no source passage is on record.

## Under the Value and Impact tests

**Cost centre by default; product at a security vendor.** At most employers security is funded as risk reduction — the austerity-shaped reasoning the [hub](dream-job-hub.md) screens against. It passes the Value and Impact tests at an employer whose *product* is trust — certificates and PKI, identity, device security, secure communications — or in a regulated device business where security is part of the approval and the brand. The owner's cryptography roots make the first kind unusually natural.

## The gap, and the shortest path

**The gap is leadership of a security function, and the credentials a hiring panel screens for.** Nothing in the record shows him owning a security programme, running incident response, leading security engineers, or holding a current security certification; the "official title" is, in his words, twenty-six years old.

1. **Take the security lead on the product in front of him** — the device threat model, the scanner programme, the patch SOP's operation — so that "owned the security of a device line" becomes a sentence the record can carry.
2. **One current credential that matches the target** (a security-architecture or security-management certification), chosen for the employer shape rather than collected.
3. **Target the product-is-trust employers first**, where the cryptography background is an asset rather than a curiosity.
