## Profile

Professional software engineer since 1996. Most work in C++ and Python; advanced SQL; working JavaScript and C#; intermediate Rust. Domains: embedded mobile devices, data engineering, machine learning/AI, cellular technologies, health, telecommunications, data science, security. Has applied neural networks to senior health and safety. Architect, technical team lead, and manager of teams up to 15. Leveraging AI in engineering since 2023; cares about shifting quality left and eliminating toil. [primary]

## What he is looking for

A Sr. Principal Engineer or Sr. Staff Engineer position with an employer interested in the work rather than the titles; "a perfect combination of Innovation, Value, and Impact". Has already performed Principal-level work across the Lively devices and apps product line. [primary] This positioning drives the [primary resume's](../resume/primary-resume.md) rule that outcomes outrank titles at every cut level.

**What those three words mean to him**, stated 2026-09-13 and narrower than they look. **Value** is judged from the business's standpoint, never from "will I be paid for it": an organization's alpha comes from the value it generates, every member is already assumed to contribute, and so *pulling your own weight is a form of rent-seeking* — the bar is the surplus above it. **Impact** is the differential — how much more value exists because he did the work — plus two conditions: it has to be **visible**, because at this career stage impact nobody sees cannot be afforded, and it has to sit on a trajectory **associated with the money-making parts of the organization**. Reaching people is only one narrow domain in which value can be generated, and domain is explicitly no longer his criterion; magnitude is. Underneath is a two-axis frame: **technical power** and **money power**, with his own strength squarely on the technical axis — and the observation that powerful parties who *agree* get what they want spectacularly, while the ones who disagree need lawyers. The job he wants is one where those two axes are pointed the same way. He also names the failure mode: "business as usual" work is decided by pragmatic reasoning rather than aspirational or growth reasoning, and he is currently not close enough to where his employer generates most of its revenue.

## Working identity

A builder-architect: close enough to implementation to solve the hard technical problems (positioning, concurrency, battery life, 64-bit migrations, cryptography) and broad enough to shape systems, teams, development practice, and product outcomes. Prefers empowering, self-organizing teams and "leading from behind". Repeatedly steps beyond assigned scope — performance, testability, operations, hiring. [primary] The 2007 San Diego visit, when he set out to absorb a retiring GUI lead's knowledge and become the codebase's steward, is the earliest explicit statement of that stance. [long]

**Stewardship is the first principle, and eliminating toil is how it is practised.** In his words (2026-09-16), "good stewardship and strong sense of ownership was always one of the first principles of my personal and professional life" — held as a security professional, a data steward, an engineer who inherits and hands on codebases, and a manager. The evidence register by register is [2026-09-16 stewardship as a first principle](../../raw/brag/2026-09-16-stewardship-first-principle.md); outward it is shown through examples, never recited.

**The move to the U.S. was a decision with more than one door.** Working out how to emigrate, he was talking to Irina Martimyanova about **Akvelon** — the Bellevue firm co-founded by Constantine Korovkin, whom he had briefly encountered in the IIT years — and the contract work for **Microsoft** that route implied. What he took instead was the Salford Systems offer to join them in San Diego (owner, 2026-09-16). **When is not established**: he joined Salford in 2000 but worked from Ukraine for roughly a decade, first visited San Diego in 2007, and the stages above put him American-based from about 2011, so the Akvelon conversation most likely sits near the relocation rather than near the hire — the owner has been asked. **The mailbox now dates the decision (read 2026-09-25):** on 26 April 2010 he wrote to Dan Steinberg that he and his wife "have recently made up our minds to start working on relocation from Ukraine to either Germany or U.S. or maybe some other developed country where a reliable future can be built", and that he envisioned "a senior developer position with U.S. office". Dan backed it — expansion in the U.S. was "mandatory" once the Japanese version sold — and on 3 May 2010: "the consensus here is that it would be great to have you in the office in San Diego!" So the destination was open in spring 2010, Germany included, which fits the Akvelon conversation sitting near that decision. A working visit to San Diego in autumn 2009 (onboarding the new GUI developer, localization, source control and CI) preceded it ([2009-06-01](../../raw/brag/2009-06-01-spm-release-engineering-japanese-localization.md)). Either way it is the earliest instance of the pattern below: the offer he took had a product and a long horizon rather than a contracting route. Akvelon recruiters are still looking at his profile in 2026.

**Long tenures, by temperament.** Seventeen years at Salford Systems and eight-plus at GreatCall/Best Buy Health. The owner reads this as self-knowledge rather than inertia: watching a Salford colleague who was plainly primed for Silicon Valley's job-hopping culture, he saw early that he was not, and "my 10 years of tenure tendency is the best evidence" (2026-09-16; see [2012-06-01](../../raw/brag/2012-06-01-spm7-linux-port-tbb-evaluation.md)). It is also why the career reads in the decade-long stages above.
