# Made and defended staffing recommendations for the Mirabit outsourcing team across SPM 8.2, SPM 9.0 and CloudSML

## What I did

Made and defended staffing recommendations for which developers to retain across **SPM 8.2**, **SPM 9.0** and **CloudSML** as the company weighed outsourcing-vendor (**Mirabit**) headcount changes, escalating the case up to leadership — "You guys need to make the case with Steve O." Named individuals in the thread: Bogdan, Nicolai, Vlad.

> **Grounding needed — owner, 2026-09-16 — and what the mailbox then showed.** The owner was "pretty sure this is an outfit from Poland and they were building SPMQt under my full management and architecture authority". The mailbox separates two vendors:
>
> - **Mirabit is Ukrainian.** Its domain is `mirabit.com.ua`; its people write with Ukrainian-locale dates, and one mentions travelling to Kharkiv. Named in the mail: **Vladyslav (Vlad) Frolov** (CloudSML lead), **Bohdan Khorolets** (the "Bogdan"), **Nikolay Golikov** (the "Nicolai"), **Maksym Skoryk** (UA QA team lead), **Alexander Kravchenko** (SPM GUI fixes the owner reviewed and merged) and **Anna Kulyk**. Several also held Salford addresses in early 2017. The staffing thread is **"Re: Mirabit", 20 April 2017** — the owner to Dan Steinberg: keep the developers for SPM 8.2 and CloudSML, because they will be needed to maintain SPM 8.2, build SPM 9.0 and continue CloudSML; "We work with Bogdan and Nicolai for several months already… Keeping them will be a great support for Vlad"; Dan: "You guys need to make the case with Steve O"; the owner then asks whether to have Dawn [Keller, of Minitab] bring Steve O into the conversation. The UA QA team was still testing into December 2017 under Minitab. **So the entry's 2015 start is not supported; the evidence is 2017.**
> - **The Polish vendor was Milo Solutions** (Warsaw; founded 2010, Qt desktop and embedded specialists — [company](https://www.milosolutions.com/en/who-we-are/), [Qt team](https://milosolutions.com/en/team/qt)). The mailbox has Milo's developer working the `spmqt` macOS deployment with the owner (February–March 2017) and Milo invoicing "Health Care Productivity" (Salford's legal name) for February 2017, **invoice approved by the owner**. Alongside, the owner ran the weekly SPM 8.2 and SPM-Qt team meeting, set a merge-request-per-day policy for the `spm-qt` developers, and reviewed their merge requests; Dan asked in January 2017 to cut SPM-Qt to "life support" at 10% of the owner's time.
>
> The owner's recollection — a Polish outfit building SPM-Qt under his management and architecture authority — therefore fits **Milo Solutions**, and is consistent with the resume's claim that he was Chief Architect and Product Owner of the Qt rewrite. It is a separate relationship from this Mirabit staffing case and is not yet its own entry. Both identifications await the owner's confirmation.

> **Second owner statement, later on 2026-09-16 — the vendor identity is now genuinely open.** "Mirabit might be unrelated to Salford Systems. Maybe it is related to Akvelon and potential contract work for Microsoft. I talked to Irina Martimyanova about it, and Akvelon founded by Konstantin Korovkin, who I briefly encountered during IIT era."
>
> **The mailbox disagrees, and both records stand until the owner resolves it.** What the Gmail search shows is not ambiguous: `mirabit.com.ua` addresses appear throughout the Salford Systems correspondence of 2017 — on SPM 8.2 weekly meetings the owner chaired, on CloudSML progress reports, on a UA QA team thread that continued under Minitab into December 2017, and in a licence request and merge-request reviews. The staffing thread that this entry is built on is titled "Re: Mirabit" and is between the owner and Dan Steinberg. On that evidence Mirabit *was* a Salford outsourcing vendor.
>
> Two readings fit both accounts, and the owner is the only one who can choose:
>
> 1. **The name is right and the memory of Poland belongs elsewhere** — Mirabit was the Ukrainian vendor; the Polish Qt work was Milo Solutions; the Akvelon conversation was a separate, earlier matter about moving to the U.S.
> 2. **Two things share one name in memory** — a Mirabit-related Akvelon/Microsoft contracting possibility around 2000, and the Ukrainian vendor of 2017, which happen to be remembered together.
>
> Nothing in this entry may be told outward until this is settled; the staffing behaviour it records (defending a team's continuity to leadership) is safe, the vendor's identity and nationality are not.
