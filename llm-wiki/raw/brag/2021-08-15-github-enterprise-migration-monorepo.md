# Spearheaded the company-wide migration from Atlassian tooling to GitHub Enterprise

## What I did

Spearheaded the move from Atlassian-hosted source control to GitHub Enterprise across the engineering organization. The embedded team's **monorepo** was the hard case: large, with a long history and build tooling that assumed the old host, and the platform/DevOps group was occupied elsewhere and had little help to offer for it.

Rather than let the team's migration stall behind someone else's queue, I took ownership of the process end to end — researched what the monorepo migration actually required, worked out the mechanics, and carried it through to completion.

## Why it matters

The engineering organization landed on one source-control and automation platform instead of straddling two, and the embedded team was not left behind on the old one while the rest moved. The migration also became the foundation the later build and packaging work stood on: the **GitHub Actions** automation that produced uniquely-numbered package builds for the Conan work in 2022 existed because this migration had happened first (see *Related*).

This is also an example of a pattern I repeat deliberately: when a change everyone agrees is right has no owner, I take the ownership rather than wait. I put myself forward as the human face of raising engineering standards, which made it easier for others to commit to the effort — people generally want things to get better but are reluctant to stick their heads out first.

## Skills demonstrated

Source-control platform migration, monorepo handling, developer-experience ownership, self-directed research on an unfamiliar migration path, organizational influence without formal authority, seeing a cross-team change through to completion.
