# Raising the bar in every environment, and reading which environments let it compound

## What I did

In the owner's words: **"Bar raiser mentality … accurately reflects my own personal philosophy of always striving to improve and exceed expectations."** The practice is constant; what changes is the environment, and reading the environment correctly is the other half of the skill.

**Salford Systems — the bar rose and the team rose with it.** The outsourced Ukrainian team **over-performed**: given a higher standard and the support to reach it, they became instrumental contributors to product features and quality rather than a capacity buffer.

**The Qt GUI rewrite — surprised, then willing.** That team was *surprised* to be asked to aim higher, and then mostly happy about it. Worth remembering as the common case: people rarely resent a raised bar, they resent an unexplained one.

**Minitab — a mixed bag, and the more valuable for it.** The bar did not rise everywhere or evenly. That short tenure is what taught the owner to read an environment rather than assume one, and it is precisely why it matters as the step immediately before GreatCall.

**Best Buy Health — friendly, supportive, and careful.** Nobody discouraged him; the environment was genuinely supportive. But the tendency ran increasingly to *tread carefully*, and too often people settled into a **rent-seeking** posture. The owner's own framing, 2025-05-28: "I'm a self-assigned bar raiser of the team… I'm the opposite of rent seeking. Rent seekers are perfectly fine by themselves — they wouldn't be rent seekers if there weren't opportunities. I'm looking for opportunities to bring value to the company."

**Current Health — the practice had a name.** That organization ran a formal **"Bar Raiser"** role per service. The owner found it on meeting one of its engineers who held it, and recognised his own philosophy in someone else's job title: "Bar raiser describes my dream role very well. I love learning, I love mentoring and I happen to be talented in Risk Management."

## Why it matters

- **It is the through-line of the leadership record**, which the resume otherwise shows as scope rather than as behaviour.
- **The claim is unusual and defensible**: not "I have high standards", but *I raise the bar in whatever environment I land in, and I can tell which environments let a raised bar compound*. One is a personality claim; the other is judgement, and it has four employers of evidence.
- **It explains career decisions** without anyone having to criticise an employer.

## Sensitivity

**The characterisation of Best Buy Health — "tread carefully", rent-seeking — is T1 and never goes outward.** It is an honest private assessment of a current employer's culture and would read as disloyalty in a room, whatever its accuracy. The outward version is the owner's own behaviour and his judgement about environments, which needs no comparison and names nobody. See [sensitivity tiers](../../wiki/workflows/sensitivity-tiers.md) rule 8 — capture is not disclosure.

## Skills demonstrated

Raising standards without authority; reading organizational culture; mentoring; self-assessment; choosing environments deliberately.
