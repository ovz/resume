# Argued early that the Lively Home smart-home vision was out of touch, and learned what a poorly defined mission costs even a well-funded company

## What I did — the owner's account (2026-09-24, verbatim excerpt)

> that's my leaders usually right story - every blunder I saw coming and some of them I could even voice against; Livey Home which was visioned as full smart home solution sounded out of touch, incurred non-trivial waste and was one of dominating factors to pivot towards osterity aka profitability; I expressed my expert opinion early and it fell on deaf ears; That was my learning of what poorly defined missions does to even rich organization; We weren't general magic but MVNO on top of Verizon cannot be run on a shoestring budget, so some of that rich company pshyco complex was present until goodwill repairment ran out

The full note, which also carries the percentiles story, is preserved in [2022-11-10](2022-11-10-percentiles-over-averages-location-statistics.md). "Leaders usually right" is the owner's shorthand for the leadership principle *Are Right, A Lot*.

## What the record shows

- **Lively Home was GreatCall's home-sensor offering** — wireless sensors on doors, refrigerators and cabinets, installed by Geek Squad, marketed alongside the phones and wearables after Best Buy acquired GreatCall (press coverage at the time; one source could not be re-fetched on 2026-09-24).
- **His contemporaneous reflection, April 2023,** in a one-on-one agenda: *"In my uninformed opinion it would be productive to hold to the fire the feet of those who squandered the budget on Lively Home, Watch, Meds, or whatever it was that lost a ton of money and made Best Buy Health unprofitable. Folks on Smartphones and us were doing a good job and we are doing a good job helping to reduce costs of the call center."*
- **His later reading, August 2026,** in the same channel: GreatCall "fell victim" to the success of other Best Buy businesses, and the exclusive-brands organisation tried to fit the Lively device line into its own way of working rather than learn how to build custom devices better than any competitor.

## Why it matters

The lesson is the one he names: **a poorly defined mission wastes money even in a rich organisation.** A device-and-service company running as a mobile virtual network operator on Verizon needs real investment — it is not a shoestring business — but investment without a sharp mission bought products that did not fit the customer, and the correction, when it came, was austerity. For the owner it became a screening question about employers ([dream-job hub](../../wiki/dream-jobs/dream-job-hub.md) § *Mission, belonging and ownership*) and an example of raising bad news early.

## Skills demonstrated

Product judgement from the engineering seat; raising a concern early; reading an organisation's mission and funding model; drawing a durable lesson from an outcome he could not change.

## What was blocked, cut short, or wrong

- **The objection did not change the decision.** He says his opinion "fell on deaf ears". That is the honest ending, and it is why the telling ends on what he learned and what he now asks employers, not on the decision.
- **"Every blunder I saw coming" is the owner's characterisation** and is not told outward in that form; it reads as hindsight to a listener.
