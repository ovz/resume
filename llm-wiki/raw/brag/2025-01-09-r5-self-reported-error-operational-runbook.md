# Translated R5 self-reported-error telemetry into an operational runbook

## What I did

Co-authored and drove operational follow-through for the R5 self-reported-error runbook, translating device-health telemetry and syslog evidence into guidance that Operations could use. The process covered production monitoring, proactive investigation, device-specific diagnosis, physical-device recovery, vendor escalation, Care Operations and NOC coordination, and preparation of evidence for a leadership decision about whether a war room was needed.

Participated in the January 8 review of the Lively Wearables Operational Runbook, which was updated with a six-month review cadence. On January 9, followed up to confirm that the detailed R5 self-reported-error guide had also been reviewed and would be published in its controlled location, keeping the supporting diagnostic guidance aligned with the main operational runbook.

## Why it matters

The guide recognized that an elevated error count does not necessarily explain the underlying device condition. It gave responders a path from detection to engineering analysis and recovery prioritization, including recovery of devices that could clarify root cause or support a better solution. It also connected proactive identification of non-operational devices with customer-care readiness while accounting for the non-trivial cost of physical-device recovery.

The documented review process helped keep operational guidance current without assuming that every device generation required a wholesale rewrite. The evidence confirms the January 2025 review, publication follow-through, authorship, and documented response model; it does not quantify investigation time saved, devices recovered, or incident outcomes.
