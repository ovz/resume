# Devices I built firmware for have sat on Best Buy shelves since 2019 — Lively Mobile+ (R4) and Lively Mobile2 (R5)

## What I did

In the owner's words (2026-10-01; spelling normalized, the grounding request elided):

> New brag item about the presence of Lively Mobile, Lively Mobile+, Lively Mobile2 and possibly other incarnations of R4 and R5 devices since 2019 physically on shelves in Best Buy store. This added powerful reinforcement of my meaningful work for Best Buy Health. I could come to the store, watch customers interacting with products, talk to store associates up to and including Store general manager about the work I do. … This will make a line or two in my achievements as it differentiates from the bulk of even embedded software engineers that didn't see their prominence physically on shelves, or even 1M confirmed active lines of services in data warehouse.

The accomplishment is not a single deliverable. It is **seven years of shipping embedded software into a retail consumer product** — the device the owner writes firmware for is a boxed SKU in a national retail chain, bought off the shelf by seniors and their families, and every generation he worked on reached that shelf:

- **Lively Mobile+ (R4)** — launched 23 April 2019, five months after he joined; sold in Best Buy and Walmart stores from April 2019 (per the CPSC record), relaunched after the August 2019 recall, and still listed at Best Buy in December 2025.
- **Lively Mobile2 (R5)** — launched 11 March 2024. Best Buy's own product page carries the model number **R5-RTL-BLK-8IN**: the internal generation name is literally on the retailer's listing. Still listed in June 2026.

**What the shelf gave him, in his own account:** he could walk into a Best Buy store, watch customers handle the product, and talk about the work with store associates — up to and including a store general manager. For an embedded engineer that closes a loop that usually stays open for a whole career: the firmware's customer stops being a requirement document and becomes a person in an aisle.

### Follow-up, 2026-10-01: where in the store it stood

In the owner's words (spelling normalized):

> I have definitely seen Lively Fab in Best Buy. It was on the periphery of the store, but in the front line next to Apple, Amazon, Google etc.

"Lively Fab" is his name for the Lively display; what the word stands for is an open question for him (see *Evidence limitations*). The public record supports the substance: an industry analyst firm, **Wave7 Research**, reported that Best Buy added **two aisle endcap displays dedicated to Lively (GreatCall) products in the first quarter of 2020** — one for phones, one for wearables — and that in **early August 2021** Lively had **taken over a majority of the store's main prepaid display space** at a Kansas Best Buy, with a source saying the stronger presence would roll out broadly. Wave7's principal, Jeff Moore, described it as "huge display space at Best Buy". That places Lively in the store's main wireless area — the brand-display front line — rather than in an aisle at the back.

## Why it matters

- **Rare for embedded engineers.** Most embedded software ships inside something the engineer never sees sold — a module, a subsystem, an industrial controller, a white-label board. Having a consumer SKU of one's own work on a national retailer's shelf, across two device generations and seven years, is uncommon and immediately concrete to a listener.
- **Scale on the public record.** GreatCall had **more than 900,000 paying subscribers** when Best Buy bought it in 2018 (Best Buy's own press release and SEC 8-K); an independent reviewer puts it at **approximately one million customers** across GreatCall's services. That is the service the R4 and R5 devices are part of.
- **Motivation, honestly stated.** The owner's own framing is that seeing the product in the store was a *reinforcement of meaningful work* — evidence that the device he works on is real, bought, and relied on by vulnerable people. That is the same instinct as his line that "radio waves and technology stacks are deprived of a sense of urgency to save a human life".
- **Stakeholder reach beyond engineering.** Conversations with store associates and a store general manager are the retail end of the same product; it is a small but genuine example of talking about technical work to a non-technical, customer-facing audience.

## Skills demonstrated

Embedded software delivered as a retail consumer product; sustained delivery across two device generations; communicating technical work to retail staff and store leadership; customer empathy from direct observation.

## Evidence limitations

- **The store visits and the conversations with associates and a store general manager rest on the owner's statement alone.** No date, store, or name is recorded. *(To supply if wanted: which store, roughly when, and whether a conversation led anywhere.)*
- **R5 in a physical store is now adequately supported, though less directly than R4.** The 2024 launch release says "Lively.com and BestBuy.com", not "Best Buy stores". But Lively's own store locator (captured December 2025) offers Lively Mobile2 "in person" at local stores, the NCOA review says a Lively device can be bought in-store, and the owner saw the display himself. *Sold in stores* is supportable for both generations. What is not public is which retailer chains the locator returns for Mobile2 — the capture is the page, not a search result.
- **"Next to Apple, Amazon, Google" is the owner's observation, not a public fact.** Wave7 Research places Lively in the store's *main prepaid display space* and on aisle endcaps; no public source found names the neighbouring brands. Outward text may say "the store's main wireless display" on Wave7's authority; the neighbours stay his first-person memory. Wave7's August 2021 observation is one Kansas store, with a source predicting a broad rollout — not a confirmed chain-wide layout.
- **"Lively Fab" — the owner's term, meaning not yet confirmed.** Possibly a fixture or brand-display name used in Best Buy or Lively retail; no public source uses it. Recorded verbatim; not used outward until he says what it stands for.
- **The 2020 "endcap display" sentence is ambiguous on its own, and Wave7 resolves it.** "Look for it on the new endcap display at Best Buy stores" follows a sentence about Lively Mobile Plus at Walgreens. Wave7's account of the same quarter is one endcap for Lively phones and one for wearables. In Q1 2020 the Lively wearables were Lively Mobile Plus and Lively Wearable2, so the wearables endcap most likely carried R4 — an inference, since Wave7 does not name the devices. Cite Wave7 for the endcaps, not the ambiguous sentence.
- **"One million" in public sources counts customers or subscribers across all GreatCall services, phones included** — not R4 and R5 devices alone, and not "active lines". The owner's own figure (below) is the internal one.
- **Review counts on BestBuy.com are a weak proxy for sales.** Some reviews may be syndicated from the manufacturer's site. They show a live, reviewed product, not a sales number.
- **Device identity of the other "incarnations".** The original **Lively Mobile** (model SW100, listed at Best Buy by May 2018, [capture][bby-lm-2018]) predates the owner's 26 November 2018 start date, so it is not his work. **Lively Wearable2** (SKU 6339071, [capture][bby-wearable2-2021]) is a Bluetooth pendant paired with a smartphone, not an R4 or R5 device, as far as the public record shows. Neither is claimed here unless the owner confirms a role in it.

### The one-million active lines — tier case, recorded per rule 7

The owner states he has seen roughly **one million confirmed active lines of service** in the enterprise data warehouse, whose line-of-service table he worked with directly (see [the warehouse entry](2022-05-18-snowflake-edw-device-telemetry.md)). He calls it a true fact.

[Sensitivity tiers](../../wiki/workflows/sensitivity-tiers.md) put an internal metric's *value* at T2, and the figure here is the owner's recollection rather than a dated artifact. Conservative reading applied: **the internal count is not the outward claim.** The outward claim uses the public figures — "more than 900,000 paying subscribers" (Best Buy, 2018) or "approximately one million customers" (Caring.com) — which make the same order-of-magnitude point without disclosing anything. The owner's recollection is recorded here because the round number already matches the public figures and adds nothing an outsider could not find; whether that makes it T1-safe in general is a policy question for the owner, recorded in the maintainer tracker.

## What was blocked, cut short, or wrong

- **The first R4 retail run lasted about a month.** CPSC's record has Lively Mobile Plus sold in stores from April to May 2019, then recalled on 30 August 2019. The shelf story and the recall story are the same story; the product came back, and it was on BestBuy.com from March 2020 to the end of 2025. See [the recall entry](2019-08-30-r4-cpsc-recall-and-relaunch.md).
- **R4 appears to have left retail by December 2025.** The last capture still lists it but carries sold-out / no-longer-available text. Say "2019–2025" for R4, not "since 2019", unless the owner knows otherwise.
