# Prepared and delivered the technical due-diligence presentation for Minitab's acquisition of Salford Systems

## What I did

Directly asked by the company's president, **Dan Steinberg**, to prepare and help deliver the technical presentation — development process, infrastructure, product roadmaps and internals — for **Minitab's** visiting representatives ahead of and around the acquisition in November 2016, alongside colleague **Vlad Frolov**.

## What the mailbox shows (read 2026-09-25)

- **8 November 2016 — the brief.** Straight off a call with Dan, the owner briefed Vlad: Minitab's representatives would visit the next week to see "our software development process, infrastructure and assess how we can integrate". He set the strategy in the same email — *"I think the ideal is to persuade Minitab that CloudSML is _the_ way to integrate with us"*: cloud-style deployment (Rancher), the API server as the integration point, a web app third parties could offer their clients — "If we are building something non-trivial we need a platform for that. CloudSML is such a platform." Minitab had asked for three things: the development process, product roadmaps, and product internals, including **a code-review session with their architect** across all three codebases (the desktop engine API, SPM-Qt and CloudSML).
- **15–16 November — the visit.** Minitab preparation took 60% of his week. He and Vlad held a joint slide-editing session on CloudSML; on the 16th he prepared for and ran the "technical deep dive" with Minitab's team at the San Diego office. He sent the two decks afterwards — *Salford Systems Projects* and *CloudSML* — both on his own slides.com account.
- **18–20 November — the written follow-up.** Minitab's Duane Long sent remaining questions; the owner answered them in a shared document within two days, some of which "require digging into source code, version control history". Minitab's Dawn Keller: "Thank you, Oleg, for the quick turnaround!"
- **5–6 January 2017 — the second visit, into the code.** He hosted Minitab's return visit, framed as "the best way to dive into the codebase", with Vlad demonstrating the automated testing framework; afterwards he supplied the Fortran share and line counts ("Thanks again for a great visit!"). The acquisition closed and integration began in March 2017.

## Why it matters

A distinct, datable M&A-technical-diligence credibility marker: being the person the company president trusted to represent engineering practice to an acquirer. This sits usefully alongside the resume's existing Minitab-era material (the CI/CD migration to Visual Studio Team Services, onboarding cut to under a week) as the earliest datable point in that relationship — the moment before the acquisition closed, not the integration work after it.

## Skills demonstrated

Representing engineering practice and infrastructure to an acquiring company's technical diligence team; being trusted by company leadership with an external-facing, high-stakes presentation; articulating development process and product roadmap to an outside technical audience.
